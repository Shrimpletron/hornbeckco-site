What every packet includes built backward from the meeting question

A packet you skim, instead of a model you re-derive.

  • Decision calendar — the real deadlines in play: enrollment windows, RMD onset, filing dates, conversion cutoffs.
  • Data-gaps table — every hole in the file paired with the exact ask that closes it. A guessed answer never substitutes for a real one.
  • Scenario writeups — stated assumptions, each carrying a direction-of-error tag so you can see which way a miss cuts.
  • Sensitivity bands — longevity, spending, and a deterministic stress path. Your planning software's Monte Carlo stays yours; this feeds it.
  • One-page client summary — plain terms, meeting-ready.
  • Facts ledger + runnable math script — every figure traceable to a source document or a line of code. Nothing typed by hand.
  • QA & methodology note — what was checked and how, written for your compliance file.
  • Sign-off lines — every advice-bearing item requires your individual sign-off, so your file shows an active decision on each one.

How an engagement runs the clock pauses on gaps

Six questions in. One packet out.

  1. Intake gateSix questions before anything starts: who the client is (anonymized), the question the meeting must answer, materials, your assumptions and the bridge-funding answer, your agenda, the meeting date.
  2. Gap-chaseMissing or conflicting facts come back as a list — each gap with the exact ask that closes it. The turnaround clock pauses until they do.
  3. BuildScenarios run in code against dated, primary-sourced constants. The script ships with the packet, so nothing is a black box.
  4. QAEvery load-bearing figure machine-diffed against the engine output; every ledger value cold-read against its source. The process is periodically tested by seeding a deliberate error and confirming the checks catch it.
  5. Advisor sign-offThe packet arrives as your draft, in your branding, labeled for advisor review. You own the advice; the sign-off lines keep that boundary in your file, not just in the fine print.

Anonymized at intake. Enforced, not promised.

Hornbeckco does not accept identified client data.
